Validation tracker

Excel, Power Query
Built in week 6, still in use

A live tracker that replaced a manual daily count

Situation
Invoice validation volumes across a group of LATAM markets were being counted by hand each day, which made the daily figure slow to produce and easy to get wrong.
What I did
Built a single workbook that grouped markets into two zones, pulled the raw extract in through Power Query, and calculated volumes and aging with live formulas instead of re-typed totals. Set it up so a refresh replaced the whole manual step.
Result
The daily figure became a refresh rather than a recount, and the team gained visibility of which markets were building a backlog before it showed up in the aging report.

Defect escalation

Cloud invoice capture tool
Two defects, both resolved

Two recurring system faults, evidenced and escalated

Situation
A cloud document-capture tool was intermittently mishandling certain document types. The symptom looked like ordinary keying error, so it was being corrected invoice by invoice rather than reported.
What I did
Logged each occurrence with the document type and conditions until the pattern was clear enough to distinguish a system fault from human error, then raised it through the support workflow with reproducible evidence rather than a description.
Result
Both defects were accepted and fixed, which removed a recurring rework loop for every processor on the queue rather than only for me.

The lesson I took from it: three identical mistakes in a week are rarely three mistakes.

Controls I work to

Accounts payable is where cash leaves the company, so most of the job is knowing which steps are not negotiable.

  • Three-way match integrity. Purchase order, receipt and invoice agree before posting, or the invoice waits.
  • Duplicate detection before entry. Checked on reference, and again on vendor, amount and date.
  • Separation of duties. The person who processes invoices does not create or amend vendor master records.
  • Bank detail changes verified independently. Confirmed on a number already held on file, never one supplied with the request.
  • Tolerances applied, not stretched. Anything outside the threshold goes to the buyer with the numbers attached.
  • Invoice date and posting date kept distinct. Wrong dates distort aging, due dates and period cut-off.
  • Approval matrix respected on non-PO spend. An urgent invoice is still an unapproved invoice.
  • Rejections given a reason and a record. So the pattern is visible and the vendor can actually fix it.

How I read the wider cycle

My queue sits in one part of procure-to-pay, but most exceptions are caused upstream of it. Knowing where the fault started is usually how it gets fixed.

A price variance is a purchase order problem. A missing receipt is a warehouse problem. Both arrive as an invoice problem — so the fix usually needs the person upstream, with the evidence already gathered.
